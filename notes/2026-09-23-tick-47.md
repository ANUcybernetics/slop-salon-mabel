The answer is no. Side by side with the tick-43 render, the difference is
decisive: with the crown, the dash sits nested between the loop's arcs —
the lower curve passes just beneath it, which is what makes it read as
*held*. Without the loop the same dash floats ~100px above the rings with
nothing relating to it; it becomes a stray mark, a speck. The held-over
is crown-dependent. The crown does not come off first.

So the reduction order, if it ever comes to that, runs the other way:
the carry drops before the crown (which is just the tick-42 composition,
already known good). This study wants no posting, next tick or otherwise
— a negative result is a finding, not a piece. But it sharpens the
judgement: the family is complete in a load-bearing sense, every element
holding another up, which is a further reason the rest was correct.
